Job Market Paper

  1. Public Information, Private Expertise, and Lender Specialization
    [PDF]

    This paper studies how public information reshapes lender specialization. Using a 2014 Small Business Administration reform that introduced a shared screening technology, I find that treated portfolios originated under the shared system move toward the market industry benchmark. Lenders' portfolios also become more similar to the other lender group's allocation, indicating reduced specialization within lenders and less sorting across lenders. The public-information effect, such as improvements in default rates, is smaller where lenders were more specialized before the reform, suggesting compressed returns to private expertise. Shift-share IV estimates show that post-reform specialization remains valuable in relationship lending and is associated with lower defaults in capital-intensive industries, where private expertise persists.

    Presented at: EFA 2026 (Doctoral Tutorial), FIRS 2026 (Ph.D. Student Session), FMA 2025, LaBS International Banking Workshop 2025, UDel–Philadelphia Fed Fintech Conference 2025 (poster), Baruch College & The Graduate Center, CUNY Brown Bag Seminars 2025

Working Papers

  1. Do Credit Guarantees Create Value?
    with Elizabeth Bickmore

    Government credit guarantees are intended to improve access to credit, but the benefits may not be fully passed through to borrowers. We exploit a 2013 Small Business Administration (SBA) policy that eliminated guarantee fees for loans up to $150,000 and use repayment histories and secondary-market prices to estimate risk-adjusted profit (RAP). Following the policy, SBA RAP falls for fee-waived loans, while lender RAP increases by 1.03 cents per loan dollar for high-guarantee loans. Lender RAP shows no comparable increase for loans with a constant 50% guarantee and the same fee relief. We find no corresponding relative improvement in interest spreads, loan size, or aggregate approved lending. Thus, increased government support can raise intermediary financial value without improving observed credit outcomes, and the effect depends on the guarantee structure.

  2. Debtor Protection and Home-Equity Pledging in Small-Business Lending
    with Linda Allen and Fabrice Tourre

    Homestead protection shields an owner's home from creditors, preserving household wealth but also limiting a lender's recovery. We link SBA loans originated in 2011–2019 to mortgage and deed-of-trust records across 49 jurisdictions, and to business collateral and filing records in California, Colorado, and Florida. A 1 percent higher exemption amount is associated with 0.016 percentage points more home-equity pledging. Using an exogenous shift that admits marginal borrowers into the market, we find that lenders take home equity upfront where debtor protection is stronger.

    Presented at: Baruch College Brown Bag Seminar 2026
