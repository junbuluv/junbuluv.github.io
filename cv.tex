\section{Job Market Paper}
\vspace{-0.25in}
\begin{outerlist}
    \item \href{https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5445275}{Public Information, Private Expertise, and Lender Specialization} \\
    {\small\textit{Presented at: EFA Doctoral Tutorial (2026); FIRS Ph.D. Student Session (2026)}}
    \par\vspace{1mm}
    % Abstract for the job market paper only; other papers stay title-only.
    {\small This paper studies how public information reshapes lender
    specialization. Using a 2014 Small Business Administration reform that
    introduced a shared screening technology, I find that treated portfolios
    originated under the shared system move toward the market industry
    benchmark. Lenders' portfolios also become more similar to the other
    lender group's allocation, indicating reduced specialization within
    lenders and less sorting across lenders. The public-information effect,
    such as improvements in default rates, is smaller where lenders were more
    specialized before the reform, suggesting compressed returns to private
    expertise. Shift-share IV estimates show that post-reform specialization
    remains valuable in relationship lending and is associated with lower
    defaults in capital-intensive industries, where private expertise
    persists.\par}
\end{outerlist}
